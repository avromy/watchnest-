## Design and usability judgment

The reviewed product convincingly presents a warm premium family library. Cream surfaces, deep green type and navigation, restrained gold actions, soft collection colors and matching line icons create a coherent identity. The large child play/continue action is easy to distinguish from favorite and overflow actions. Rounded cards and restrained shadows support grouping without making every surface compete.

Parent navigation separates Overview, Library, Requests and Family, with legible secondary destinations. Add videos remains prominent where appropriate. The dashboard makes a pending request and each child's library status easy to scan. Assignment uses named child chips with explicit checkboxes and a counted approval action. Preview and manage controls are adjacent to their videos. Collection editing and sharing remain understandable on the phone.

Child Home, Library and Discover are visibly distinct and consistently placed. Portrait and landscape iPad layouts preserve readable titles, generous video targets, favorite buttons and a clear active destination. Library search offers a useful Ask Parent recovery when nothing matches. Discovery cards use Ask Parent rather than a play affordance. Request confirmation and pending status explain what happens next. PIN error and search focus screenshots show a conspicuous gold focus outline; errors are explained in text rather than color alone.

Phone layouts reflow successfully in the inspected states: Parent cards become single-column, form controls remain comfortably sized, and child navigation moves to a labeled bottom bar. Desktop layouts use additional columns without losing the same component language. I found no supported material clipping, broken grouping, unreadable primary text, collision of essential controls, or visibly inaccessible essential action in the inspected pixels.

Player loading, authorization failure, startup failure, paused, expired and ended states have understandable messages and visible recovery/navigation. This is a judgment about the rendered states only, not their real triggering behavior.

## Material defects

**None supported by the inspected evidence.** No repair is required by this visual verdict.

## Optional concerns, not acceptance blockers

1. **Long Parent phone administration pages.** In `390x844/parent-controls-middle-1.png`, `parent-details/parent-discovery-permissions-enabled-middle-3.png` and `middle-4.png`, repeated per-child instructions and stacked session/discovery cards require considerable scrolling. A compact section jump or less repeated explanatory copy could make busy-parent administration faster. The visible controls themselves remain clear and usable.
2. **Library content starts fairly far down the iPad landscape page.** In `1024x768/child-library-top.png`, the header/navigation, search/filter region and large Collection card occupy the first viewport before the video grid. Reducing this vertical footprint could expose video choices sooner. Search and Collections are useful visible destinations, so I do not judge this a material task obstruction.
3. **Preview close control scrolls out of the visible panel.** Comparing `1024x768/parent-preview-dialog-full.png` with `parent-preview-dialog-internal-bottom-full.png`, the Close preview button is visible at the top but absent from the internal-bottom state. Keeping the close action visible could reduce return scrolling. These screenshots do not establish a trapped dialog or failed dismissal.
