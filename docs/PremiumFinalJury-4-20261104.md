## Assessment

The inspected presentation meets the intended warm family library direction. Cream surfaces, deep green, restrained gold, soft illustration colors and recognizable profile animals form a coherent identity. Type hierarchy is strong, buttons are readable, and generous spacing feels deliberate. The child home combines a prominent continue-watching action with direct Library/Favorites/Discover shortcuts. The Parent interface has a stable four-part navigation and clear secondary sections. Child and Parent modes feel related while their tasks remain distinct.

Phone, portrait and landscape tablet, desktop and intermediate-width captures preserve readable controls and meaningful grouping. Phone child navigation remains visible at the bottom, with adequate end-of-document clearance in inspected library/discovery bottoms. The two additional 360×800 y500 views visibly show all three Home shortcuts and both first Library thumbnails with their left/right edges, titles and neighboring spacing readable; those elements are not being inferred from metadata or an obstructed first viewport.

The approval selection clearly distinguishes video selection from “Who can watch?” assignment. Preview, collection sharing and inbox actions use explicit labels. The observed phone approval success message confirms that a video was approved. Errors, PIN feedback, empty states, paused sessions, time expiry and player startup failure are readable and give an exit or recovery direction. The synthetic player surroundings remain calm and readable. Their synthetic content cannot establish genuine embedded-player usability.

## Finite findings

No supported material design/usability rejection was found in the inspected evidence.

1. **Optional density improvement:** `ci139-candidate/test-results/visual-evidence/candidate/390x844/parent-details/parent-approval-assignment-full.png`, 390×844. Parent approval requires considerable vertical travel past a large title and intake region before the assignment and preview controls. This is still visibly ordered and usable; reducing introductory space after results resolve would make repeated phone approval faster. This is a polish preference, not a rejection.
2. **Optional recovery-copy improvement:** `ci139-candidate/test-results/visual-evidence/candidate/768x1024/child-library-error-full.png`, 768×1024. “Please try again” is followed by “Choose profile,” rather than an explicitly labeled Retry action. The exit is clear, but a more direct label or explanation would better match the suggested next step. This does not obstruct recovery sufficiently to reject the design.

## Limitations

This verdict assesses supplied static synthetic Chromium renders of a local production build. It is not interactive workflow verification, a complete accessibility audit, real child testing, authenticated hosted acceptance, security validation, real YouTube playback validation, physical iPad/Safari/PWA evidence or release approval. Fixture artwork and repeated synthetic titles limit judgment about arbitrary real thumbnails and long-content diversity. I inspected representative major families and widths, not every raster in the packet. Some long full-document captures were used for overall flow and supplemented with readable viewport slices; no tiny full-document rendering was used as the sole basis for fine-text judgment.

The before folder was not needed and was not used as a frozen-layout standard. PASS does not close any separate real-device, playback, functional or security gate.
